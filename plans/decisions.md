When a decision changes a plan, the plan's text changes in the same commit.

**N1. The never-cut set does not fit the week as the steps are drawn** (p5 B1).
*Background.* The execution chain has five merges in series after P0 (PV → E3a → E3b → E4 → E6), with the two largest steps, E3a and E4, on it, and E3a also waits for E2. Each merge waits for a draft, `/review-pr`, the maintainer's review, and CI. The reviewer's schedule of 2026-10-18, assuming half-day reviews and no slips: Mon 19 #586 (once its conflict is resolved), P0b, #587, and PL merge, while S0 and PV are built; Tue 20 S0, E0, E1, and PV merge, and S2a, E2, and E3a start; Wed 21 S1 and S2a merge; Thu 22 E3a and E2 merge, E3b and S2b continue; Fri 23 S2b and E3b merge, and E4 starts; Sat 24 S3 and S4 merge; Sun 25 E4 merges late at the earliest, and **E6, a never-cut step, cannot start before E4 merges, so it cannot merge**. (2) Shrink the never-cut set: make E6 and E4's **Performance** row off-ramps. (3) Both.
*Recommendation.* (3): restructure as in (1), put the **Performance** row in E4b, and make E4b the off-ramp cut just before S4b, since a measurement can fail for reasons outside the step's control.

### The execution design

**N24. What `vectorized=True` receives for shared and record arguments** (p3 M8).
*Background.* A vectorized callable "accepts every array argument with an extra leading axis". Whether an argument every evaluation point shares is stacked or passed once depends on the call (in `forward(theta=prior, data=y)` only `theta` varies), which the callable cannot know; and an input whose spec is a `RecordSpec` is one input.
*Options.* (a) Every array leaf of every argument is stacked to the chunk's length, shared ones as read-only `np.broadcast_to` views, so nothing is copied; a record argument is stacked leaf by leaf, and a derivative receives the same stacking. (b) Shared arguments are passed unstacked, which needs a per-call declaration the callable cannot make.
*Recommendation.* (a).

**N25. A record-valued output from opaque code, and the check relation** (p3 M9).
*Background.* `pure_callback` requires the host to return exactly the pytree of its `ShapeDtypeStruct`s. The plans do not say whether an opaque body returns a `Record` or may return a mapping, whether the per-point check is its own notion or the declarations plan's **conforms** (§ 2.1, which admits a same-kind cast and any dtype when none is declared), or whether the keyword form `OutputSpec(forward=T)` checks the bare array or `{forward: …}`.
*Options.* (1) "Each output must conform (`declarations-and-addressing.md` § 2.1) to `output_spec.term_spec`; a base output is the bare array, never `{name: array}`. A record output may be a `Record` or a mapping with exactly the spec's fields, converted to a `Record` in canonical order. A conforming output is cast to the declared dtype, warning once per call when the cast loses precision; one that does not conform is an `invalid` failure." (2) A separate check of tree structure, shape, and dtype.
*Recommendation.* (1), so the two plans share one relation (directive 11).

**N27. The `jax` executor before compiled lifting, and `batch_size`** (p3 M17).
*Background.* Before compiled lifting (which joins the core only if time allows), the plan does not say whether `jax` runs an eager `jax.vmap`, which registers tokens at once under registration rule 5, or a stored JAX program. `batch_size` maps with `lax.map`, a staging trace, so an opaque call inside a traceable lifted body would stage token 0 and warn wrongly.
*Options.* (a) E3a's `jax` runs the lifted body through the program store, which is compiled lifting, and compiled lifting joins the core. (b) E3a's `jax` runs an eager `jax.vmap`; `batch_size` arrives with compiled lifting after the workshop and is refused until then.
*Recommendation.* (b), which is smaller and keeps tokens correct through rule 5.

**N28. `parallel_chain_map` always `vmap`, with a `chain_map` method option** (p1 M8; p3 M4).
*Background.* 6.10, item 6 makes `parallel_chain_map` always use `vmap`, with `method_options={"chain_map": "pmap"}` to opt into one chain per device, so the device count never changes the draws unasked. No step delivers it, and it changes draws on multi-device machines (a silent change).
*Options.* (1) Assign it to E4 (or E4b), whose runners become module-level JAX programs, with a test ("with 4 CPU devices and 4 chains, the default draws equal those with 1 device; `chain_map="pmap"` runs one chain per device"). (2) Defer it, and drop `chain_map` from 6.16's first worked example.
*Recommendation.* (2), since it is not on the core path.

**N29. Evaluation points of a callable that returns a law, a function, or a batch, and the order of S4b and E5** (p1 M5) [B.6, S4b, pending note; `execution-layer.md` § 6.4 rule 7, § 6.7, E5].
*Background.* G1b lifts a law-returning function into draws `{law_of_y: law}`, and S4b has sweeps store their element spec; E5 moves lifting and sweeps onto `evaluate_at_points`. The per-point check is defined only for arrays, `EvaluationPoints.output_spec` is `None` for these callables, and nothing says whether `jax` may `jit(vmap(f))` a law-returning `f`, whether a law is pickled to a `process` worker, or who assembles the outcomes and stores S4b's element spec.
*Options.* (a) A callable whose output spec does not describe a value a host callback can return (`execution-layer.md` § 6.2) always runs on a Python executor (`sequential`, `thread`, or `process` when its outputs pickle), never on `jax`, and its outcomes are checked by conformance of each output's term spec rather than by shape; E5's done-condition adds "lifting `law_of_y` over a law and the tour's `predictive` example still work under `sequential`; a sweep's result keeps the element spec its maker stores"; and README says S4b and E5 may merge in either order. (b) E5 leaves law-, function-, and batch-returning lifts on today's path and moves only numeric ones.
*Recommendation.* (a).

### Declarations

**N31. G1a: the library's laws in an interim whole form over a record become positional or wrapped** (p2 M14) [B.7, row 3; B.6, G1a item 2].
*Background.* G1a item 2 says these laws (`_posterior_declaration`, `make_posterior`, and `_marginal` in `_empirical.py`) become "positional or wrapped". The two forms offer different names (`beta` and `sigma`, or `params`) and different draws (`{beta, sigma}` or `{params: {...}}`), a public contract left to the implementer per site.
*Options.* (1) Positional everywhere: the draws stay unchanged, and the law offers the names its draws hold. (2) Wrapped under today's name: names are kept, but every draw gains a level.
*Recommendation.* (1), with "`make_posterior` of a target declared `OutputSpec(params=RecordSpec(...))` returns a posterior whose event is `OutputSpec(RecordSpec(...))`" in G1a's done-condition.
